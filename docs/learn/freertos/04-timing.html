4. Timing: vTaskDelay vs vTaskDelayUntil main/tasks/sensor_task.c:71

"Run every 10 ms" and "wait 10 ms" are two different things.

Build it from first principles

Time is a tick counter

The kernel counts ticks since boot (xTaskGetTickCount()). A delay moves the task to the delayed list with a wake-up tick; the tick interrupt moves it back to Ready when that tick arrives.

vTaskDelay: relative to now

Wake-up = the tick at which you call it + N. The work before the call adds to the period. With work wₖ in pass k:

start(k+1) = start(k) + wₖ + P drift after n passes = w₀ + w₁ + … + wₙ₋₁

vTaskDelayUntil: relative to the last wake-up

You keep a variable last_wake; each call wakes at last_wake + P and then advances it by P. The work no longer matters, as long as it is shorter than P:

start(k+1) = start(k) + P (if wₖ < P)
Drifts
while (1) {
    work();             // 2–5 ms
    vTaskDelay(
      pdMS_TO_TICKS(10));
}
Fixed period
TickType_t last =
    xTaskGetTickCount();
while (1) {
    work();
    vTaskDelayUntil(&last,
      pdMS_TO_TICKS(10));
}

Break it: drift

Each pass does 2 to 5 ms of work. Watch the start times against the 10 ms grid: with vTaskDelay they slide by the work time of every pass; with vTaskDelayUntil they stay on the grid.

Why these values

Why 10 ms, and how many ticks is that? SENSOR_TASK_HZ = 100 gives a 1000 / 100 = 10 ms period; at a 1000 Hz tick, pdMS_TO_TICKS(10) = 10 ticks, exact.
What if a pass is longer than the period? vTaskDelayUntil returns at once to catch up, and the newer xTaskDelayUntil() returns pdFALSE so you can count overruns.
Why does the motion task have no delay at all? Its period is set by the data: it blocks until the sensor signals a new sample. Its time step dt comes from the snapshot timestamps, clamped to 1–50 ms, so a late sample cannot make the controller jump.
Why a 50 ms sensor-lost timeout? 50 ms is 5 missed samples: long enough to ride out a busy I2C bus, short enough that at 450 mm/s the robot covers only 22.5 mm blind before it brakes.
To remember. A periodic loop uses vTaskDelayUntil(&last_wake, period). vTaskDelay means "wait at least this long", not "run every".

Deep dive: rounding

pdMS_TO_TICKS(ms) = ms × tick rate / 1000, rounded down. At 100 Hz, 5 ms becomes 0 ticks and vTaskDelay(0) does not wait at all; 15 ms becomes 1 tick. A delay of N ticks waits between N−1 and N tick periods, because the call can land anywhere inside the current tick.
